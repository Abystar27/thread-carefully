Thread Carefully
The Slow Eco Workshop
Àdìrẹ, cassava paste & community making · May 2026

In May 2026, Yíímiiká and Bridget Steenkamp of Eco Histories co-led a Slow Eco Textile Workshop at Southlands College — bringing students and staff from completely different disciplines to the same table to get their hands dirty and talk raw sustainability.

🧵
The Process
Àdìrẹ Ẹlẹ̀kọ — Starch Resist Dyeing

Using cassava paste — a staple crop grown and eaten across West Africa since the 16th century — as a starch resist, and onion skins sourced from the Southlands kitchen as natural dye, participants engaged directly with the indigenous Nigerian textile tradition of Àdìrẹ Ẹlẹ̀kọ.

The paste was applied to cotton fabric in floral patterns using stencils, then dipped in dye. When the paste was removed, the resist pattern remained — a process that has been practised in Yoruba culture for generations.

Revisiting historic techniques does more than preserve a rich cultural heritage — it shifts our modern perspective on sustainability. Slowing down to engage with these crafts forces us to appreciate the true cost and value of what we consume.

🌿
The Outcome
From Workshop to Exhibition

The fabrics created in that workshop became Space 4 — NATURE in the exhibition. The footprint pieces — made using mud, turmeric, saffron, and coffee grounds on recycled cotton — became Space 8 — Tread Lightly.

What happened in that room was more than a craft session. Students from different departments sat alongside sustainability researchers, textile artists, and community members. The conversation that started around the dye buckets carried all the way into the gallery walls.

👤
Co-led by
Bridget Steenkamp · Eco Histories

Bridget Moira Steenkamp runs Eco Histories' Slow Workshops — interdisciplinary sessions that use slow, hands-on craft to open conversations about sustainability, material history, and our relationship with what we make and consume.

Her button workshops, porcelain-making sessions, and textile dyeing practices are all present throughout this exhibition — from Space 3 through to Space 9.
